Case study

Romper

A web platform where teams narrow a long list of ideas down to the few worth building, phase by phase.

  • RoleUX/UI Designer, one of two
  • TeamDesign lead, 2 designers, devs, client stakeholders
  • Year2025
  • PlatformDesktop web
  • StatusPartly launched

Romper is a client platform built at Trinetix. I was one of two product designers, working with a design lead, developers and client stakeholders. I owned the admin dashboards, the personas page and the persona creation flow.

Problem
Teams collected ideas in canvas tools. Ideas got lost, and nobody could see which ones had the team's support.
What I did
Designed client and project dashboards and the persona creation flow, based on a client brief and three interviews.
Outcome
Dashboards approved after four iterations. Persona management moved into the platform. Partly launched.
01Context & role

Picking the ideas worth building

Romper helps teams choose which ideas to take forward. Each project moves through personas and strategy, then ideas go through three phases, and each phase narrows the list.

I was one of two designers on the product. My part was the admin dashboards, the personas page and the flow for creating a persona. The ideas board and strategy screens belong to the other designer.

02Problem & users

Ideas on a canvas, support nobody could see

Before Romper, teams gathered ideas on canvases in Miro, FigJam or Notion. Ideas got lost between boards and nothing kept them in order. It was hard to tell which ideas the team liked and which they rejected, so it was unclear which ones had real support.

  • Admin

    Runs the idea process. Sets up projects, assigns teammates, manages personas and experts.

  • Teammate

    Discusses and reacts to ideas. A project can have any number of teammates.

  • Subject matter expert

    Brings domain knowledge to the ideas.

03Research

Same product, different questions

  • Client brief

    One session with the client to set the scope.

  • 3 interviews

    One admin, one teammate, one subject matter expert.

The roles wanted different things from the same screen. Admins open Romper to check progress: where each project stands and how many ideas made it to the next phase. Teammates only want to know which projects are waiting for them. That split shaped the dashboard decisions that followed.

04Key decisions

Two decisions that shaped the product

Decision 01

A progress view instead of a control panel

The problem

Early versions treated the dashboard as a menu: shortcuts to users, clients and personas, quick actions, counters. They answered "where can I go", while admins came to find out "what is happening".

Why

Three bars show where a project is and what's left, which a single number can't. Moving management into tabs freed the main view for progress. The final version went through four iterations and was approved with stakeholders.

Decision 02

Persona creation as a step-by-step wizard

The problem

Personas sit at the center of a project: the team asks them about ideas in the side chat. Admins needed to create them inside Romper.

05Final UI

Two levels of dashboards

Each level answers one question: what's happening at this client, and what's happening in this project.

One client

Projects for one client with Personas, Strategy and Ideas progress. Personas, experts and team sit in tabs.

One project

Objectives, current status, ideas per phase and who on the team is active.

Personas

Active personas and experts at the top, available ones below. "Set journey" places a persona at a stage of the customer journey: awareness, interest or trial.

06Results

What came out of it

  • Client and project dashboards, approved after four iterations with stakeholders.
  • Persona management moved into the platform: admins create and edit personas themselves.
  • Dashboard content defined by a client brief and three interviews.
  • Part of the flows has shipped; the rest is in development.

We didn't track product metrics. I'd measure time from first idea to shortlist, the share of ideas with team feedback, and how many teammates take part in each phase.

07What I'd do differently

What I'd do differently

  1. Agree on a success metric before designing. Without one, the results here stay qualitative.
  2. Test dashboard iterations with users, not only review them with stakeholders. Four rounds would likely have been fewer.
